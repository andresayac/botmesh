Harden the connection setup and the input limits. A malformed `uri` (or one that is not `mysql://`/`mariadb://`, or holds an unencoded `#`, or an `@` after the host, the sign of an unencoded `/` or `?` in the password) now throws a `TypeError` from the constructor that never repeats the URI, instead of Node's `Invalid URL` error carrying the password in its `input`. `uri` and the discrete options combine (the discrete ones override the matching parts) instead of the URI being silently dropped. When `start()` cannot reach the server it names `host:port` and the database (mysql2 reported a refused `localhost` with an empty message), keeping `err.code` and the original error as `cause`. `tablePrefix` is limited to 43 characters (55 for `MysqlScheduleStore`), so a long prefix no longer leaves half the tables created. Ids over 1024 bytes and `ctx.user` values of 16 MiB or more fail with a `RangeError` before anything is written. A statement of 1 MiB or more runs on its own connection, closed when it fails (a statement over `max_allowed_packet` used to leave a dead connection in the pool that failed the next statement); a statement that could not be written to an already-closed connection is sent once more; and `stop()` during `start()` closes the pool being created.
